\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{ch:eval}

This chapter develops the framework used to evaluate the guidance method.
All evaluations are conducted on single-weather-step experiments, allowing us
to isolate the behaviour of the guidance procedure without the additional
effects of autoregressive propagation.

We first inspect representative guided samples qualitatively to establish an
intuitive picture of the resulting interventions (T0). We then compare
candidate flow-time guidance profiles using three complementary criteria (T1):
the multivariate propagation of the guidance effect, the spatial deviation of
its footprint from the prescribed target mask, and the variability of this
footprint across ensemble members. Target realization is treated as a
prerequisite throughout this comparison.

Finally, with the guidance profile fixed, we examine how the generated state
changes when varying the two components of the scenario specification: target
intensity and target region (T2).

The framework developed here is applied in the following chapter to select the
guidance configuration and scenario setting used for the construction of a
complete high-temperature storyline.

\section{Evaluation setup}
\label{sec:evaluation-setup}

We first introduce the notation shared by the evaluations below.

Let
\[
    \Gamma
    =
    \left\{
        \gamma^{(1)},\ldots,\gamma^{(K)}
    \right\}
\]
denote the set of candidate flow-time guidance profiles. Each
$\gamma\in\Gamma$ specifies how the guidance strength is distributed over the
residual flow.

Let $E$ denote the set of experimental settings over which these profiles are
compared. An experimental setting $e\in E$ specifies the quantities held fixed
across candidate profiles: the initial forecast context, the target mask
$\pi_e$, including its target variable $v_e^\star$, level $\ell_e^\star$, and
spatial support, and the prescribed relative intensity $\rho_H(e)$. Since all
experiments in this chapter consist of a single weather step, this intensity
directly determines the target for the generated state.

For each experimental setting, all candidate profiles are therefore evaluated
on the same scenario specification. The scalar guidance gain is adapted
independently for each ensemble member to realize the prescribed target, while
$\gamma$ determines how this guidance is distributed over the residual flow.

For each $e\in E$, we evaluate $M$ ensemble members and collect the resulting
instances in
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}
    :=
    \left\{
        (e,m)
        \;:\;
        e\in E,\;
        m=1,\ldots,M
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:evaluation-index-set}
\end{equation}
When convenient, we write $a=(e,m)\in\mathcal{A}$ for a single evaluation
instance.

A central object throughout the evaluation is the state change attributable to
guidance itself. For a guided state $x_{e,m}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}$ and its
matched unguided-under-guided-context counterpart
$x_{e,m}^{\mathrm{ung}\mid\mathrm{gui},\gamma}$, we define the
\emph{guidance effect} as
(TODO: rename to GE everywhere, I only changed it here)
\begin{equation}
    \Delta x_{e,m}^{\mathrm{GE},\gamma}
    :=
    x_{e,m}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}
    -
    x_{e,m}^{\mathrm{ung}\mid\mathrm{gui},\gamma}.
    \label{eq:guidance-effect}
\end{equation}

The two states share the same autoregressive conditioning context,
deterministic forecast, and initial residual-noise realization, and differ only
in whether guidance is applied during the residual flow. Their difference
therefore isolates the state change induced by guidance.

For analyses concerned with the magnitude and spatial structure of this
intervention, we additionally define the non-negative \emph{guidance
footprint} component-wise as
\begin{equation}
    G_{e,m,v\ell ij}^{\gamma}
    :=
    \left|
        \Delta x_{e,m,v\ell ij}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}
    \right|,
    \qquad
    \ell\in\mathcal{L}_v.
    \label{eq:guidance-footprint}
\end{equation}


\section{T0: Qualitative evaluation}
\label{sec:qualitative-evaluation}

Before quantitatively comparing the candidate guidance profiles, we first
inspect representative guided samples qualitatively. The aim is to establish
an intuitive picture of how guidance modifies the generated weather state.

We examine the convergence of the targeted quantity during the residual flow,
the distribution of the guidance effect across atmospheric variables and
vertical levels, and its spatial structure across the flow for representative experimental
settings.

These diagnostics are used for interpretation only and do not enter the
quantitative comparison developed in T1.



\section{T1: Guidance-profile evaluation}
\label{sec:guidance-profile-evaluation}

\subsection{T1.1: Multivariate propagation of guidance}
\label{sec:multivariate-propagation}

The first criterion examines how strongly an intervention targeted at a
particular variable, level, and region propagates through the atmospheric
state. We evaluate this response within the target region, outside the target
region, and over the complete spatial domain.

Because the target region is part of the experimental setting $e$, we define
\[
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{mask}}^e
    :=
    \{(i,j): k_{ij}^e=1\},
    \qquad
    \mathcal{R}_{\neg\mathrm{mask}}^e
    :=
    \{(i,j): k_{ij}^e=0\},
\]
and let $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{global}}$ denote the complete spatial grid. In
the following,
\[
    r\in
    \{\mathrm{mask},\neg\mathrm{mask},\mathrm{global}\}
\]
denotes one of these spatial domains.

In addition, why not define some $\mathcal{G}$ instead)
For an evaluation instance $(e,m)\in\mathcal{A}$, variable $v$, level
$\ell\in\mathcal{L}_v$, and guidance profile $\gamma\in\Gamma$, we define the
total guidance effect over region $r$ as
\begin{equation}
    A_{e,m,v\ell}^{r,\gamma}
    :=
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_r^e}
    G_{e,m,v\ell ij}^{\gamma}
    =
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_r^e}
    \left|
        \Delta x_{e,m,v\ell ij}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}
    \right|.
    \label{eq:absolute-guidance-response}
\end{equation}

To characterize the response of a variable as a whole, we aggregate over all
levels on which that variable is defined,
\begin{equation}
    A_{e,m,v}^{r,\gamma}
    :=
    \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_v}
    A_{e,m,v\ell}^{r,\gamma}.
    \label{eq:variable-guidance-response}
\end{equation}
For surface variables, $\mathcal{L}_v=\{0\}$, while for upper-air variables
the sum extends over all available pressure levels. The level-resolved
quantities $A_{e,m,v\ell}^{r,\gamma}$ are retained separately to inspect the
vertical distribution of the response.

Because the magnitude of the guidance effect differs substantially across
atmospheric variables, we normalize each variable relative to the candidate
profiles evaluated at the same target intensity. Let
\[
    E_{\rho}
    :=
    \left\{
        e\in E :
        \rho_H(e)=\rho
    \right\}
\]
denote the experimental settings with final relative intensity $\rho$. For
each variable, region, and guidance profile, we compute the response averaged
over experimental settings and ensemble members,
\begin{equation}
    \overline{A}_{v}^{r,\gamma,\rho}
    :=
    \frac{1}{|E_{\rho}|M}
    \sum_{e\in E_{\rho}}
    \sum_{m=1}^{M}
    A_{e,m,v}^{r,\gamma},
    \label{eq:pooled-variable-guidance-response}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
    A_{v,\max}^{r,\rho}
    :=
    \max_{\gamma'\in\Gamma}
    \overline{A}_{v}^{r,\gamma',\rho}.
    \label{eq:guidance-response-normalization}
\end{equation}

The \emph{multivariate propagation score} for an individual experimental
setting and ensemble member is then
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{prop},e,m}^{r,\gamma}
    :=
    \frac{1}{V}
    \sum_{v=1}^{V}
    \frac{
        A_{e,m,v}^{r,\gamma}
    }{
        A_{v,\max}^{r,\rho_H(e)}
    }.
    \label{eq:multivariate-propagation-score}
\end{equation}

Normalizing each variable independently prevents variables with intrinsically
larger responses from dominating the comparison and gives equal weight to each
atmospheric variable. A larger $S_{\mathrm{prop}}^{r,\gamma}$ indicates a
stronger multivariate guidance response in region $r$ relative to the other
candidate profiles evaluated at the same target intensity.


\subsection{T1.2: Spatial deviation from the target mask}
\label{sec:spatial-mask-deviation}

The propagation score introduced above measures the magnitude of the guidance
effect, but not its spatial shape. We therefore examine how closely the
guidance footprint on the targeted variable--level channel follows the
prescribed target mask.

For an evaluation instance $(e,m)\in\mathcal{A}$ and guidance profile
$\gamma\in\Gamma$, we restrict the guidance footprint to the targeted variable
and level,
\begin{equation}
    G_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
    :=
    G_{e,m,v_e^\star\ell_e^\star ij}^{\gamma}
    =
    \left|
        \Delta x_{e,m,v_e^\star\ell_e^\star ij}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}
    \right|.
    \label{eq:target-guidance-footprint}
\end{equation}

To compare spatial shape independently of the overall magnitude of the
intervention, we normalize this footprint to sum to one,
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{G}_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
    :=
    \frac{
        G_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
    }{
        \displaystyle
        \sum_{i',j'}
        G_{e,m,i'j'}^{\gamma}
    }.
    \label{eq:normalized-guidance-footprint}
\end{equation}

The target mask $\pi_e$ is already normalized by construction. Restricted to
its targeted variable--level channel, it defines the spatial distribution
\[
    \pi_{e,ij}
    :=
    \pi_{e,v_e^\star\ell_e^\star ij},
    \qquad
    \sum_{i,j}\pi_{e,ij}=1.
\]

We measure the discrepancy between the normalized guidance footprint and the
target mask using total variation distance,
\begin{equation}
    D_{e,m}^{\mathrm{TV},\gamma}
    :=
    \frac{1}{2}
    \sum_{i,j}
    \left|
        \widetilde{G}_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
        -
        \pi_{e,ij}
    \right|.
    \label{eq:guidance-footprint-tv}
\end{equation}

Since both terms are normalized spatial distributions,
\[
    D_{e,m}^{\mathrm{TV},\gamma}\in[0,1].
\]
A value of zero indicates that the guidance footprint exactly matches the
spatial distribution of the prescribed mask, while larger values indicate
increasing spatial deviation. Such deviations may arise because the guidance
effect extends beyond the target region or because it is distributed
non-uniformly within it.

For each experimental setting and guidance profile, we report the mean and
standard deviation of $D_{e,m}^{\mathrm{TV},\gamma}$ across ensemble members.
Lower values indicate closer spatial alignment between the guidance footprint
and the target mask.

Because the footprint is normalized before computing the distance, this
criterion is independent of the overall magnitude of the guidance effect and
therefore complements the multivariate propagation score in
\Cref{sec:multivariate-propagation}.


\subsection{T1.3: Ensemble variability of the guidance footprint}
\label{sec:guidance-footprint-variability}

The preceding criterion characterizes the spatial shape of the guidance
footprint, but not how consistently that footprint emerges across ensemble
members. We therefore quantify the variability of its local magnitude across
stochastic realizations.

For a fixed experimental setting $e$ and guidance profile $\gamma$, we consider
the footprint on the targeted variable--level channel,
\[
    G_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
    =
    \left|
        \Delta x_{e,m,v_e^\star\ell_e^\star ij}^{\mathrm{gui},\gamma}
    \right|.
\]
We compute its pixel-wise standard deviation across the $M$ ensemble members,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{e,ij}^{\gamma}
    :=
    \operatorname{std}_{m}
    \left(
        G_{e,m,ij}^{\gamma}
    \right).
    \label{eq:guidance-footprint-pixel-spread}
\end{equation}

This produces a spatial map of the ensemble variability of the guidance
footprint. Unlike the normalized footprint used in
\Cref{sec:spatial-mask-deviation}, its magnitude is retained, such that
$\sigma_{e,ij}^{\gamma}$ remains expressed in the physical units of the
targeted atmospheric variable.

To summarize this variability within the prescribed target region, we apply
the area-weighted masked-average operator introduced in \Cref{ch:specify},
\begin{equation}
    S_{e}^{\mathrm{ens},\gamma}
    :=
    \mathcal{M}_{\pi_e}
    \left(
        \sigma_{e}^{\gamma}
    \right).
    \label{eq:guidance-footprint-ensemble-spread}
\end{equation}

A smaller $S_{e}^{\mathrm{ens},\gamma}$ indicates that the local magnitude of
the guidance footprint is more consistent across ensemble members, while a
larger value indicates stronger dependence on the stochastic realization.
The metric requires at least two ensemble members.

For each guidance profile, we compare
$S_{e}^{\mathrm{ens},\gamma}$ across experimental settings and prescribed
intensities. This criterion complements the preceding evaluations by measuring
ensemble variability rather than the overall strength or spatial alignment of
the guidance effect.


\subsection{T1.4: Profile comparison and selection}
\label{sec:guidance-profile-selection}

The three criteria are considered jointly when comparing guidance profiles:
larger multivariate propagation indicates a stronger coupled atmospheric
response, smaller total-variation distance indicates closer spatial alignment
with the target mask, and ensemble variability quantifies the dependence of the
guidance footprint on the stochastic realization. Together with the qualitative
diagnostics from T0, these measures form the basis for selecting the guidance
profile $\gamma^\star$ in the following chapter.



\section{T2: Scenario-specification analysis}
\label{sec:scenario-specification-analysis}

With the guidance profile fixed, we next examine how the generated state
depends on the scenario specification. We vary the two components introduced
in \Cref{ch:specify}: the prescribed intensity $\rho_H$ and the spatial support
of the target mask $\pi$.


\subsection{T2.1: Target intensity}
\label{sec:target-intensity-analysis}

We first vary the prescribed intensity $\rho_H$ while keeping the target mask
$\pi$ and guidance profile $\gamma^\star$ fixed. For each intensity level, we
inspect the resulting guided state and guidance effect to assess how the
magnitude and spatial structure of the intervention change as the target is
moved further from its unguided reference.


\subsection{T2.2: Target region}
\label{sec:target-region-analysis}

We next vary the spatial support of the target mask $\pi$ while keeping the
target variable, level, prescribed intensity, and guidance profile
$\gamma^\star$ fixed. The compared masks share a common center but differ in
their spatial extent. We inspect the resulting guided states and guidance
effects to assess how the intervention changes when the same target is imposed
over regions of different size.


Together, T0--T2 define the evaluation framework used in the following chapter
to compare candidate guidance profiles and characterize the sensitivity of the
method to the prescribed scenario.
