\section{Flow matching}

Flow matching \parencite{lipman2023flow} models a data distribution $p_1$ as the endpoint of a
continuous transport of a tractable prior $p_0=\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Let $(p_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ be a
\emph{probability path} with these endpoints, generated by a time-dependent \emph{velocity field}
$v_t$ through the flow
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\phi_t(x) = v_t\!\big(\phi_t(x)\big), \qquad \phi_0 = \mathrm{id},
\end{equation}
whose push-forward carries $p_0$ to $p_t$; equivalently, $v_t$ and $p_t$ obey the continuity
equation $\partial_t p_t + \nabla\!\cdot(p_t\,v_t) = 0$. Sampling then reduces to drawing
$x_0 \sim p_0$ and integrating the flow to $t=1$.

Training regresses a network $v_\theta$ onto the field that generates $p_t$. This marginal field is
intractable, but it decomposes as an expectation of \emph{conditional} fields,
$v_t(x) = \mathbb{E}_{x_1 \sim p_{1\mid t}(\cdot\,\mid x)}\!\big[\,v_t(x \mid x_1)\,\big]$, each
$v_t(\cdot \mid x_1)$ generating a simple path from $p_0$ to a fixed datum $x_1$. Flow matching
exploits the fact that the tractable \emph{conditional} objective
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\; x_1 \sim p_1,\; x_t \sim p_t(\cdot\,\mid x_1)}
    \big\lVert v_\theta(x_t, t) - v_t(x_t \mid x_1) \big\rVert^2
\end{equation}
shares its gradient with the intractable marginal one \parencite{lipman2023flow}. Under the linear
(optimal-transport) path $x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1$ with $x_0 \sim p_0$, the conditional field is the
constant displacement $v_t(x_t \mid x_1) = x_1 - x_0$, so training amounts to least-squares
regression onto $x_1 - x_0$.

At inference we draw $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and integrate $\dot{x}_t = v_\theta(x_t, t)$ with a
few Euler steps; independent draws yield independent samples. Since this map from $x_0$ to $x_1$ is a
single deterministic and differentiable trajectory, its output can be steered by differentiating a
target loss through the flow---the mechanism on which our guidance builds (\Cref{ch:guide}).
